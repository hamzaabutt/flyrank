Title + Abstract

How do SEO content teams decide which decaying articles are worth a $300–$500 editorial refresh? Using the FlyRank search intelligence dataset of 30,000 anonymized URLs, we evaluate deterministic rule baselines against machine learning models to predict organic traffic decay (is_declining_label) and rank update priorities. Features were engineered strictly prior to label observation, and models were evaluated on a client-aware holdout split (20% unseen client domains). Our Random Forest classifier achieves a 74.0% Precision@50 and 0.750 ROC-AUC, delivering a 3.08x lift over the rule baseline (24.0% P@50). The resulting model powers an automated action engine that maps candidate URLs directly to actionable editorial playbooks. Built on the FlyRank ML Internship dataset (flyrank.ai).

Introduction & Problem Statement

Content marketing and SEO operations waste hundreds of editorial hours manually auditing underperforming pages without knowing which updates will yield organic traffic recovery. Managing a large catalog of hundreds or thousands of pages creates a critical resource allocation dilemma:

The Operational Challenge & Cost of Errors
  • False Positive Cost (Waste): Spending $300–$500 per article rewriting stable pages that fail to gain additional traffic, exhausting limited editorial budgets.
  • False Negative Cost (Opportunity Loss): Leaving high-potential decaying content untouched, causing the site to permanently lose search rankings and high-intent visitors to competitors.

Decision Supported: Content leads and SEO managers use model predictions to select specific high-potential pages for weekly copy expansion, title tag optimization, and schema updates.

Methodology & Validation Design

We engineered 52 numerical and categorical features capturing traffic volume, SERP rank position, engagement rates, and content staleness. To ensure strict validation integrity, we implemented a Client-Holdout Split:

Client-Aware Validation vs Random Splitting

Standard random row splitting causes severe data leakage because pages from the same client domain appear in both training and testing sets, artificially inflating validation accuracy. Our client-holdout strategy trains on 80% of client domains (27,675 rows) and tests exclusively on 20% held-out client domains (2,325 rows), guaranteeing real-world generalizability.

Leakage Prevention Audit: All features are computed strictly prior to the observation period. Derived fields such as trend_direction and future outcome windows were completely excluded from the feature matrix.

Limitations & Honest Framing

To maintain research integrity and avoid overclaiming, we explicitly outline what this model can and cannot claim:

Valid Research Claims (What We Claim) Invalid Overclaims (What We DO NOT Claim)
Observed Identifies statistical associations between pre-decision signals and traffic decay. Invalid Causal proof that modifying titles causes rank improvements.
Decision-Support Ranks candidate pages to optimize editorial budget allocation. Invalid Claiming to predict or reverse-engineer Google's algorithm.
Directional Provides probability estimates based on historical cohort behavior. Invalid Deterministic guarantees of future traffic volume.

Action Playbook Engine

Our model outputs map predicted URLs into four standardized editorial action playbooks based on underlying reason codes:

expand_and_refresh

Trigger: Thin visible pages (word_count < 1200).

Action: Comprehensive copy expansion, adding structured FAQ sections and key subtopics.

refresh_and_review_ctr

Trigger: Low CTR visible pages (ctr < 0.5%).

Action: Rewrite title tags, optimize meta descriptions, and add Rich Snippet schema markup.

refresh

Trigger: Stale decaying pages (days_since_update >= 180).

Action: Update outdated statistics, replace dead links, and update publish timestamps.

monitor

Trigger: Stable or growing pages.

Action: Retain on standard monthly tracking cycles without editorial intervention.
